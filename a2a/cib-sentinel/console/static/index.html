        What this app does

        An agent that acts on its own, and the graph that keeps it in bounds

        No person asks anything here. The Control Room Sentinel is an autonomous agent in a corporate and investment bank. It wakes on a schedule, reads the events of the knowledge graph, and when a new mandate or a new trade order appears it starts a mission by itself. Every request it makes carries its own identity, so the agent, not a person, is the subject the gateways admit or refuse.

        Its duty is the information barrier between the private side of the bank (advisory, which holds confidential deal information) and the public side (trading). When the bank takes a mandate on a company, that company goes on the restricted list and open orders in its securities are put on hold.

        How it works

        1. Something feeds the graph. An upstream system writes a mandate or an order to the IndyKite knowledge graph. On this page the four cards of the upstream feed play that system.
        2. The platform publishes the change. The project's event sink sends every audit event, including that write, to a Google Cloud Pub/Sub topic.
        3. The Sentinel wakes and reads the wire. On its schedule it pulls its subscription. A new mandate or order starts a mission; everything else is shown and ignored.
        4. It decides its next move. A language model is given a goal and rules, the event, and what happened so far, including what was refused, and chooses one move: a task, a hand-over to a person, or the end. The Sentinel can also follow a written procedure instead.
        5. It acts as itself. Each task is one plain sentence naming the records it acts on, such as "put company brightwater-pharma on the restricted list". It goes to a fleet of agents, every one behind an Agent Gateway. The Sentinel presents its own access token from Auth0; the Token Service turns it into a delegation that records each agent it passes through.
        6. The graph decides. At every hop the platform is asked whether this subject, through this chain of agents, may go on. What is allowed is written in the graph, not in the agents. A refusal is final for that task: the Sentinel moves on or hands over, it does not try another way round.

        The boundaries

        1 Admission
        Is this chain of agents a workflow the Sentinel may trigger? The deal room's gateway turns it away: only the advisory desk's workflow passes there.
        2 Capability
        Which tools does that workflow give the last agent? The Sentinel may place a hold but is never offered the tool that releases one.
        3 Intent when switched on
        Does the tool call fit what the task set out to do, and may the Sentinel do it to the resource the call names? A write passes only if the task's intent says so, and the platform decides on the order to hold before the query runs.
        4 Graph
        Does the data sit inside the Sentinel's coverage? A mandate booked in a region it does not cover is invisible to it, and a hold on an order in an unrestricted company writes nothing.

        Reading the page

        Header
        The Sentinel's identity, what decides its moves, where its events come from, its schedule, and how many tokens Auth0 issued against how many were reused. A click opens its own token.
        Upstream feed
        A click writes an event to the graph.
        The wire
        Every message the Sentinel pulled. The one that starts a mission is marked; a click opens it.
        Missions
        Each task with the Sentinel's reason, its hops, what was refused and the answer; with intent on, also the intent it was read as and what the gateway had the platform decide. A click on a task shows it on the wall.
        The wall
        The fleet on top, the graph below, the barrier between the two sides. It draws one task: its hops through the fleet, numbered, then the nodes it touched. Beside the last gateway, the tools the Sentinel is granted and those withheld from it. The four tiles below say which boundary the task passed or hit. Amber was fed, green was written by the Sentinel.
        Audit
        The records of the gateways, the Token Service and the agents. The full view shows every record, untruncated, with a search.
        Reset
        Removes what the feed and the Sentinel wrote, never the provisioned graph. Refused while a mission runs.

        How a task and a mission end

        executed
        The fleet carried the task out.
        refused
        A gateway said no: the request never reached the agent or the tool behind it.
        graph said no
        The request was allowed to travel, and the graph returned or wrote nothing for this agent.
        failed
        A technical error on the way.
        finished
        The mission: nothing is left to do.
        escalated
        The mission: a person has to take over, because a necessary step was blocked, the task budget ran out, or no next move could be decided.

        With intent on

        The stack can also run with the Intent Agent. The first gateway then reads each task as an intent: a list of questions in the vocabulary of the graph's policies, such as "may this agent restrict this company?". A task that cannot be put in those words is not passed on, and its card says no intent: this happens when a task tells the fleet which id or reason to give a new record, or describes a record through another one instead of naming it. The intent travels inside the delegation token to the agents that carry out the task.

        Three hops later, the gateway in front of the tools checks every call against it. A tool that writes passes only if the task's intent says so, and when the call names a resource, such as the order to hold, the gateway asks the platform for a decision before the call goes through. This is the fourth tile under the wall, Intent. A mission card shows both the intent carried in the token, with a decision per question asked by this console, and what the gateway itself had the platform decide.

        Why it matters

        An agent that works without a person still needs an identity, limits and a record. Here the agent has its own identity in the graph, its limits are relationships in that graph that can be changed without touching the agent, and every decision about it is recorded, by the gateways as it happens and by the platform on the wire.
